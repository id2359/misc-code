Mathematical Foundations (Smirnov RIME Trilogy)

1. RIME Baseline Equation (Paper I)

The visibility matrix $\mathbf{V}_{pq}$ for baseline $pq$ is: $$\mathbf{V}_{pq} = \mathbf{J}_p \mathbf{B} \mathbf{J}_q^H$$ where $\mathbf{B} = \frac{1}{2} \begin{pmatrix} I+Q & U+iV \\ U-iV & I-Q \end{pmatrix}$ represents the sky brightness tensor. Standard selfcal algorithms assume $Q=U=V=0$, forcing $\mathbf{B}_{\text{unpol}} \propto \mathbf{I}$.

2. Signal Generator Singularity (Paper I, §4)

Driving dipoles with identical noise ($v_X = v_Y = s(t)$) produces a wave 100% linearly polarized at 45° ($\text{Stokes } U = I$). $$\mathbf{V}_{pq} = v_{pq} \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$$ Smirnov shows that $\det(\mathbf{V}_{pq}) = 0$ (rank 1), causing irrecoverable loss of polarization information and rank deficiency.

3. Calibration Failure (Paper II, §1.1)

The gain solver attempts to fit $\mathbf{G}_p \mathbf{B}_{\text{unpol}} \mathbf{G}_q^H \approx \mathbf{V}_{\text{measured}}$.

For diagonal gains, the model predicts $M_{XY} = 0$, but injected data has $V_{XY} = v_{pq} \neq 0$. The residual $|M_{XY} - V_{XY}|^2$ cannot be minimized, causing divergence or infinite gain loop.
